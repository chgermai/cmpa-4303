The Switch That Thought It Had a Partner

January 2022 - tags: switching, virtual-networks

Two brand new switches were configured to work as a pair, except neither one actually had a partner, and the network layer that depends on that pairing quietly broke.

What Happened

During planned maintenance, new hosts placed on a particular virtual network segment could reach each other locally but nothing outside of it. Two leaf switches involved had a switch pairing feature enabled despite having no actual paired partner switch, which left an internal control interface down and broke the tunneling that made the virtual network work at all.

The Fix

Removing the leftover pairing configuration and bringing the control interface back up restored full connectivity.

Jargon, Decoded

Certain switch features assume you are always working in pairs, like a three legged race that assumes there are exactly two people tied together. Enable that feature solo and you just end up hopping in place, technically running, but going nowhere useful.

Lessons Learned

Before adding a new feature to a switch, it is worth double checking that switch is actually configured the way that feature expects. A quick pairing sanity check before go live would have caught this in minutes instead of after new hosts were already depending on it.
