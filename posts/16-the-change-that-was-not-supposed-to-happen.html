The Change That Was Not Supposed to Happen

March 2023 - tags: routing, change-management

While fixing one small, unrelated issue, someone made an edit that was never supposed to touch production, and production noticed fast.

What Happened

During a larger, planned migration of cloud networking, an engineer troubleshooting a separate and unrelated issue made a routing configuration edit outside the normal change process. The edit caused a more specific route to leak out from an older legacy environment, breaking connectivity for a publishing platform used by one of the video services.

The Fix

The change was identified and reverted quickly, and the bad route disappeared from the network within about twenty minutes of the ticket being opened.

Jargon, Decoded

In routing, a more specific route beats a general one every time, the same way a street address always wins over a zip code when a letter carrier is deciding where to drop your mail. One tiny, oddly specific route can quietly hijack traffic that used to flow somewhere else entirely.

Lessons Learned

I was just fixing something else real quick is the sentence that precedes more outages than almost any other. The fix here was not just technical, it was a full rework of how in flight troubleshooting changes get approved before they touch anything live.
