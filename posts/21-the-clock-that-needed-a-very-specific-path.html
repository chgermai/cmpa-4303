The Clock That Needed a Very Specific Path

January 2024 - tags: timing, ptp

A network cleanup removed some links that looked redundant and unnecessary on paper, except one very picky protocol needed them specifically.

What Happened

Playback issues appeared across live streaming platforms after a scheduled network cleanup removed some internal links believed to be redundant. Those particular links turned out to be required for a precision timing protocol that keeps devices synchronized to the same clock, and the remaining hardware paths were not capable of carrying that timing traffic the same way.

The Fix

The change was reverted within about an hour of the first timing alerts, and alerts cleared shortly after.

Jargon, Decoded

A precision timing protocol keeps every device on a network agreeing on the exact same instant in time, down to fractions of a second, which sounds unnecessary until you realize how many systems quietly depend on everyone's clocks matching. Removing extra links without checking what depends on them is a bit like removing what looks like a spare bookshelf, only to discover it was the one thing holding the ceiling up in that corner.

Lessons Learned

Some traffic has very specific requirements that are not obvious just from looking at a network diagram. The redesign that followed made sure enough dedicated paths were preserved specifically for timing traffic before anything else got cleaned up again.
