The Night Both Wires Under the Ocean Went Quiet

April 2023 - tags: subsea-cable, redundancy

Connectivity between two continents dropped because the backup path for an already broken cable picked the worst possible night for planned maintenance.

What Happened

Traffic between facilities on two different continents lost connectivity entirely. The primary undersea connection had already been down for some time waiting on a repair ship, and on this particular night, planned maintenance on the secondary connection took out the only path that was left.

The Fix

The vendor's operations team tried to restore the backup path remotely with no luck, then sent someone on site to physically power cycle a piece of equipment, which brought connectivity back.

Jargon, Decoded

A wavelength, in undersea cable terms, is basically one lane of light carrying data across the ocean floor. Redundancy means having more than one lane, which only helps if somebody remembers not to schedule roadwork on every lane at the same time.

Lessons Learned

A temporary single point of failure has a way of becoming permanent right up until the day it fails. Also, checking for other planned maintenance before you schedule your own is a five minute calendar look that saves hours of continent spanning outage.
