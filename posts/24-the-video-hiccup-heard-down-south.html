The Video Hiccup Heard Down South

October 2025 - tags: video, change-management

A routine network change briefly took a few hundred live channels off the air, which is a fun way to find out how many live channels you actually have.

What Happened

A change to a network circuit serving one of the data centers knocked out publishing for a few hundred live channels for about twenty minutes. Nobody was trying to break anything, the change was a normal piece of network maintenance, it just happened to land somewhere it should not have.

The Fix

The team reverted the change as soon as the impact was clear, and channels started coming back almost immediately. As a longer term move, the busiest channels were shifted to a different, healthier data center so they were not putting all their eggs in one fragile basket.

Jargon, Decoded

A circuit, in this context, is just a dedicated data path between two points, think of it as a private highway lane reserved for your traffic only. When that lane gets closed for maintenance and someone forgets to check who else is driving on it, you get a very fast, very public traffic jam.

Lessons Learned

When a piece of infrastructure has quietly become load bearing for hundreds of things at once, small change and harmless change stop meaning the same thing. Also, a same day emergency call with a name like Discussion in the title is never actually just a discussion.
